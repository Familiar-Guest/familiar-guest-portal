# Baja Beachhead: Feature Strategy

<div class="subtitle">US & Canadian owners renting in Todos Santos · Los Cabos · La Paz</div>

<p class="lead">Narrowing the launch to US/Canadian homeowners who own and rent property in Baja California Sur sharpens the product. These owners already rent on Airbnb and build a base of return guests — the perfect direct-booking candidate — but the cross-border nature of their business creates friction Airbnb papers over. That friction is exactly where Familiar Guest can win.</p>

<div class="highlight-box">
<p><strong>The core insight:</strong> for this segment, the differentiators are not generic booking features — they are <strong>cross-border</strong> features: money that moves cleanly between countries, trust strong enough to send a deposit abroad, two languages, and tools for owners who run their home from 2,000 miles away.</p>
</div>

---

## Market Analysis: The Baja California Sur Corridor

The three towns form a single, connected second-home market on the southern tip of the Baja peninsula:

| Town | Character | Owner & guest profile |
|---|---|---|
| **Los Cabos** (Cabo San Lucas + San José del Cabo) | Established resort market, highest rental rates, large STR inventory | Heavy US/Canadian ownership; mix of resort tourists and repeat visitors |
| **La Paz** | State capital, authentic, growing, calmer waterfront | More long-stay and value-oriented; growing expat base |
| **Todos Santos** | Pueblo Mágico — artsy, boutique, fast-growing, high-end | Dense US/Canadian second-home ownership; design-conscious return guests |

Todos Santos sits roughly an hour north of Cabo San Lucas and an hour southwest of La Paz — the three are a natural cluster to serve as one beachhead.

**Why these owners are ready to go direct:** they already have a season of repeat guests, they feel Airbnb's ~15.5% fee acutely on high-value bookings, and many already coordinate informally (email, WhatsApp) with returning guests. They need a trustworthy way to take that relationship direct — without becoming a tech company.

## The Cross-Border Realities That Drive Features

| Reality | Consequence | Feature opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| **Money crosses borders** | Guests pay USD/CAD/MXN; owners often want USD payout to a US/Canada bank while the home is in Mexico. FX and payout control are real pain. | Multi-currency pricing + flexible cross-border payouts |
| **Foreign ownership = fideicomiso** | Coastal property (within ~50 km of shore) is held by foreigners via a bank trust *(fideicomiso)* or Mexican corporation, not direct title. | Ownership verification that accepts fideicomiso / corp documents |
| **Taxes get complex going direct** | Airbnb auto-withholds Mexican ISR/IVA for hosts. Direct, the owner inherits ISR, IVA, and the BCS lodging tax (ISH), plus US/Canada reporting. | Cross-border income & tax helper (with strong caveats) |
| **Snowbird stays are long** | Many guests stay weeks to months in high season. | Weekly/monthly rates + installment payments |
| **Owners are absentee** | They live in the US/Canada; the home runs on local caretakers and cleaners. | Caretaker role + remote/digital check-in |
| **Two languages** | Owners are English-first; many guests (esp. Mexican nationals) are Spanish-first. | Bilingual booking page + AI-translated messaging |
| **Real infrastructure quirks** | Baja homes deal with water delivery/cisterns, blackouts, dirt/4×4 roads. | Baja-specific house-manual templates |

## Recommended Features — Stack-Ranked for This Beachhead

Scored for the Baja segment: **Owner value · Guest value · Differentiation · Probability of success.** Tags mark whether each is <span class="tag-new">new</span> versus an extension of the existing roadmap.

### 1. Multi-Currency Pricing & Cross-Border Payouts <span class="tag-new">new</span>
Show prices in the guest's currency (USD/CAD/MXN); let the owner choose payout currency and destination bank (US, Canada, or Mexico). Transparent FX, no surprises.

**Why #1:** this is *the* defining cross-border feature. Airbnb gives owners almost no control here and poor effective FX. Solving clean, predictable money movement between three countries is the single biggest reason this segment would switch — and Stripe Connect supports the rails.

### 2. Bilingual Booking & AI-Translated Messaging <span class="tag-new">new</span>
English/Spanish booking pages and guest communication, with AI translating messages both ways in real time.

**Why #2:** it instantly widens the guest base to Mexican nationals (a large, underserved direct-booking audience) while serving US/Canadian guests, and it removes the language barrier for absentee English-speaking owners. Cheap to build with AI, hard for a casual owner to do alone.

### 3. Cross-Border Trust Stack, Led Hard <span class="tag-new">new</span> emphasis
Escrow + Verified Owner + Truvi screening/damage coverage — packaged and messaged specifically as *"safe to book a home in Mexico."* (Truvi supports Mexico, unlike US-only Safely/Waivo.)

**Why #3:** the #1 emotional barrier to direct booking abroad is "am I wiring money to a stranger in another country?" Escrow (funds held until check-in) is the perfect antidote, and damage coverage is actually available in Mexico through Truvi. This is positioning as much as product.

<div class="page-break"></div>

### 4. Snowbird Long-Stay Support & Installments <span class="tag-new">new</span>
Weekly and monthly rates, plus the ability to pay a deposit now and the balance before arrival (and optionally split a long stay into installments).

**Why #4:** the Nov–Apr guest often stays weeks or months. Long-stay pricing and installments fit the actual booking behavior of this corridor and reduce friction on large reservations. Builds on the earlier "pay-in-two" idea, now segment-justified.

### 5. Remote-Owner Operations: Caretaker Role + Digital Check-In <span class="tag-new">new</span>
A limited-access role for the local cleaner/caretaker (see bookings, check-in dates, prep tasks) plus digital check-in instructions and lockbox/smart-code support.

**Why #5:** these owners are not on-site. The home runs on local help and self-check-in. Giving the caretaker a scoped view — without exposing payments or the guest list — matches how the business actually operates.

### 6. Baja House Manual Templates + Season-Timed Re-Invite <span class="tag-new">new</span> + extends roadmap
Pre-built local house-manual sections (water/cistern, drinking water, power/blackouts, 4×4 roads, nearest town, emergency contacts, useful Spanish phrases) and a re-invite that fires in Sept–Oct for the coming high season.

**Why #6:** delightful and cheap. The local-knowledge templates save absentee owners real effort and reduce guest questions; the season-timed re-invite (an extension of past-guest import) turns the predictable Nov–Apr rhythm into repeat bookings.

### 7. Cross-Border Income & Tax Helper <span class="tag-new">new</span>
Income reporting that separates US/Canada reporting (e.g., Schedule E) from Mexican obligations (ISR, IVA, ISH lodging tax), with a clear lodging-tax line item per booking.

**Why #7 (and last):** genuinely valuable — losing Airbnb's automatic tax withholding is a real worry for these owners — but it's liability-sensitive and complex. Ship informational support with firm "not tax advice" guardrails; do not over-promise. Lower near-term probability than the rest.

## How This Re-Prioritizes the Earlier Top-5

The five general features still hold, but the beachhead changes their order and framing:

- **Past-guest import (was #1)** — still essential; now paired with the **season-timed re-invite** for the Nov–Apr rhythm.
- **AI Concierge (was #2)** — folds into **bilingual AI messaging**; translation becomes its highest-value job here.
- **Double-booking radar (was #3)** — still valuable; matters most for owners cross-listing on Airbnb + VRBO.
- **Owner-owned reviews (was #4)** — useful, but lower priority than the cross-border essentials for launch.
- **Pay-in-two (was #5)** — promoted into **long-stay + installments**, now justified by snowbird behavior.

## Risks & Honest Caveats

<div class="warn">
<p><strong>Tax and legal items need professional verification.</strong> Mexican rental taxation (ISR, IVA, ISH), fideicomiso rules, and US/Canada cross-border reporting are summarized here at a strategic level. Confirm specifics with a cross-border tax attorney and Mexican counsel before building owner-facing tax features or making compliance claims. Familiar Guest should inform, not advise.</p>
</div>

- **Regulatory exposure.** Short-term rental rules in BCS municipalities can change; lodging-tax handling must be correct.
- **Payment complexity.** Cross-border payouts and FX add Stripe configuration and support overhead — worth validating early with one real owner.
- **Infrastructure reality.** Water and power constraints in towns like Todos Santos are real (confirmed in public sources) — set guest expectations in the house manual, don't hide them.
