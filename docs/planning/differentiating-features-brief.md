# Differentiating Features

<div class="subtitle">Five high-probability bets to set Familiar Guest apart</div>

<p class="lead">Five candidate features, stack-ranked for a solo founder building with AI, targeting casual rental owners — where the private Guest CRM is the moat. Each was scored on the dimensions that actually matter for this business, not a generic feature wishlist.</p>

### Scoring dimensions

- **Owner value** — does a non-professional owner with one or two properties actually feel it?
- **Differentiation** — can Airbnb, OwnerRez, Lodgify, Hospitable, or Houfy easily match it?
- **Probability of success** — build feasibility (solo + AI) multiplied by the likelihood owners adopt it
- **Moat fit** — does it strengthen the repeat-guest and trust positioning?

<div class="highlight-box">
<p><strong>The through-line:</strong> #1 and #4 feed the guest-CRM moat. #2 and #3 remove the two things casual owners dread most — the Q&A grind and double-bookings. Together they differentiate on <strong>trust and effortlessness</strong> — the real lane — not on features a channel manager could out-build.</p>
</div>

## The Ranked Five

<div class="rank-card">
<div class="rank-head">
  <span class="rank-num">1</span>
  <div>
    <div class="rank-title">Past-Guest Import & Re-Invite</div>
    <div class="rank-tag">"Jump-start your guest list"</div>
  </div>
</div>

The owner imports their existing guests — phone contacts, a spreadsheet, forwarded emails — and Familiar Guest helps them send branded "book again" invites.

<div class="rank-label">Why it's #1</div>
It solves the cold-start problem and drives the exact metric your test needs: <strong>bookings in week one.</strong> A casual owner has no audience to "market" to, but they do have a contacts list and a season of past renters. Airbnb structurally cannot offer this — it hides guest contact info. It also instantly populates the Guest CRM, your real moat. Technically it's a contacts/CSV upload plus templated Resend emails: high feasibility, high payoff.
</div>

<div class="rank-card">
<div class="rank-head">
  <span class="rank-num">2</span>
  <div>
    <div class="rank-title">AI Guest Concierge</div>
    <div class="rank-tag">Auto-answers guest questions; auto-writes the local guide</div>
  </div>
</div>

Drafts answers to guest questions ("WiFi? early check-in? parking?") from the listing and house manual, and auto-generates a local-area guide from the property address.

<div class="rank-label">Why it's #2</div>
The #1 ongoing chore for a casual owner is repetitive guest Q&A. This removes it, is genuinely AI-native (cheap for you to build, hard for an owner to replicate alone), and makes the product feel modern. Just below #1 because it improves retention rather than directly creating bookings, and answer quality needs light owner oversight.
</div>

<div class="rank-card">
<div class="rank-head">
  <span class="rank-num">3</span>
  <div>
    <div class="rank-title">Double-Booking Radar</div>
    <div class="rank-tag">Turns the iCal lag weakness into a trust feature you own</div>
  </div>
</div>

Proactively detects and alerts the owner when two synced platforms are about to collide on the same dates.

<div class="rank-label">Why it's #3</div>
A double-booking is a casual owner's nightmare — angry guest, refund, reputation hit. iCal sync is laggy by nature, and <strong>nobody markets protection against the exact gap that sync creates.</strong> You'd be the one to name it and solve it, reinforcing the "we protect you" trust angle. Ranks behind #1 and #2 because it's a safety net felt occasionally, not an everyday delight.
</div>

<div class="page-break"></div>

<div class="rank-card">
<div class="rank-head">
  <span class="rank-num">4</span>
  <div>
    <div class="rank-title">Owner-Owned Guest Reviews</div>
    <div class="rank-tag">Private testimonials the owner keeps — not a marketplace ranking</div>
  </div>
</div>

After each stay, collect a review the owner keeps and can display on their own booking page.

<div class="rank-label">Why it's #4</div>
Helps convert the "known-but-not-yet-booked" guest — a friend-of-a-friend referral — by showing real social proof, without becoming a discovery platform. Easy to build, reinforces trust and repeat-guest positioning. Ranks 4th because its value compounds over time rather than landing immediately.
</div>

<div class="rank-card">
<div class="rank-head">
  <span class="rank-num">5</span>
  <div>
    <div class="rank-title">Flexible Guest Payment (Pay-in-Two)</div>
    <div class="rank-tag">Deposit now, balance before check-in</div>
  </div>
</div>

Let guests split a larger booking into a deposit now plus the balance before arrival.

<div class="rank-label">Why it's #5</div>
Reduces sticker shock on a $2,000 week and helps close bookings. Stripe-native, so very feasible — but it's an enhancer a competitor could copy, and it doesn't deepen the moat. Solid, not strategic.
</div>

## Deliberately Not in the Top Five

<div class="callout">
<p><strong>Guest Trust Passport</strong> — a guest's good history carries to other Familiar Guest owners. The most strategically differentiating idea and a real network effect, but <strong>lower near-term probability</strong>: it needs owner density to matter, adds privacy and consent complexity, and risks drifting toward the marketplace model you've decided against. <em>Build the data rails now; switch it on later.</em></p>
</div>

<div class="callout">
<p><strong>Deposit-waiver instead of a large hold</strong> — a small fee replaces the $500 hold guests dislike. Great for casual-owner friendliness, but it depends on the Truvi / damage-coverage partnership being live, which won't happen inside the 30-day window.</p>
</div>

## Recommended Sequencing

| Phase | Feature | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| During / right after the friends test | **#1 Past-guest import** | Drives the bookings the test needs to be meaningful |
| Fast follow | **#3 Double-booking radar** | Cheap insurance against the one failure that kills trust |
| First retention release | **#2 AI Concierge** + **#4 Reviews** | Compounding moat features once owners are active |
| Opportunistic | **#5 Pay-in-two** | Turn on when a tester hits friction on a large booking |
